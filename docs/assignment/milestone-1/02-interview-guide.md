# Inclusive Public Transport Companion: interview guide

Topic: commuters report conditions to help disabled and older riders plan journeys. SDG 10 (reduced inequalities) and SDG 11 (inclusive, accessible cities).

## Consent and recruitment

Explain the assignment, voluntary participation and the right to skip questions or stop. Ask permission before recording. Collect anonymous notes; do not request diagnoses, identification documents or account credentials. Interview disabled riders and older riders directly, including people who travel independently and with assistance. Include caregivers only when the rider wants them involved, plus drivers/operators for operational questions. Do not infer needs from age or disability alone.

## Recent journey (ask before showing features)

1. Tell me about your most recent bus journey: where did you start, board and get off, and what happened?
2. What helped or prevented you from boarding, travelling and leaving the bus?
3. What information did you need before deciding whether to travel? What did you do when information was missing?

## Seating, walking and assistance

4. How did seat availability affect that journey? When is standing manageable or difficult, and what would you do if a seat was not available?
5. Tell me about the walking parts before boarding, during transfers and after getting off. Which distances, slopes, crossings, surfaces or rest opportunities affected your decision?
6. How do you describe a manageable walk: metres, minutes, landmarks, rest breaks or something else? Does this change by day, weather or fatigue?
7. What help, if any, did you need? Who provided it, when did you need to arrange it, and how did you know the request was acknowledged?
8. Which matters most when choosing between a nearer stop, a longer wait, a working ramp and a likely seat? Ask for a real example rather than assuming one universal ranking.

## Sensory needs and evidence

9. When are spoken prompts useful or disruptive? Which language, timing, volume and repeat controls would you need? Do you already use TalkBack or another screen reader?
10. What makes a journey too noisy or crowded for you? Are noise and crowding separate concerns? What evidence would make a quieter-route suggestion useful?
11. How old can a crowding/seat report be before you would stop relying on it? Ask the same separately for a working or broken ramp/elevator.
12. What would you do with an old unresolved equipment report? What would you need to see about the reporter, last check and repair before trusting it?

## Prototype tasks

Ask participants to think aloud while selecting a journey, inspecting accessibility evidence, selecting the exact bus/stop, reviewing a condition and retrying an unpublished report. Observe whether they distinguish Unknown, recent observations and unresolved faults. Do not promise seats, assistance, quiet routes, walking optimisation or spoken navigation that the app cannot provide.

## Analysis and follow-up

For each finding record the anonymous participant code, concrete journey example, expressed need, workaround, uncertainty and proposed requirement. Separate participant evidence from team assumptions. No interviews are claimed as completed by this guide. Validate required data (walkable paths and entry points, current seating observations, noise observations, operator acknowledgements) before enabling the corresponding feature.
