# Preference ownership on a shared device

High contrast and large tap targets are **device display controls**. They remain enabled/disabled across sign-out and account changes so changing riders does not remove the device's usable display controls.

Wheelchair-access and step-free requirements are **personal travel needs**. Each signed-in UID has a separate local preference record. They do not sync to another device or represent a verified mobility profile. Sign-out replaces active needs with guest defaults; signing back in reloads that UID's saved needs. Other accounts' records are not selected as a fallback. Guest changes last only for that guest session and are not copied into the next signed-in account.

The existing product defaults are retained explicitly for guests and accounts without a valid saved record: wheelchair access required **on**, step-free only **off**. These are initial search settings, not an inference about a person's disability and not a WCAG requirement. The preferences screen explains the default and asks riders to review their needs. Missing/corrupt records use the same defaults. Unsupported walking/voice/haptic/automatic-assistance/quiet-route switches remain unavailable.

Previous global wheelchair/step-free preferences have no trustworthy owner. Initialization removes them rather than assigning them to whichever rider signs in next. Existing high contrast/large-target choices are preserved. Old unsupported switches are removed. Users of older versions need to review/save their travel needs once for their account.

Startup loads the current Firebase UID before rendering. An auth-state listener handles subsequent external sign-in/sign-out/account changes; login/registration also await activation before returning to their UI, and sign-out resets needs immediately. If sign-out fails, preferences are reloaded for the still-authenticated account. Open preferences/search screens listen for changes instead of retaining the previous rider's form values. Saving during a transition captures the originating UID; queued old-account saves cannot overwrite the new account's record. Storage rejection surfaces a failure message and is not announced as a successful save.

Records remain in local SharedPreferences for later sign-in by the same UID. UID namespacing is logical isolation, not encryption or protection against a compromised device/application. The app has no account-deletion UI currently; clearing app data/uninstalling removes these local settings. Device controls and active-owner needs reset through the existing reset API without overwriting another account's needs.

Automated coverage includes separate accounts and restart, guest defaults/session-only changes, legacy migration, delayed writes across switches, rejected writes, auth-stream transitions, malformed records, reset isolation, and open preferences/search screens. Physical shared-device sign-out/login and accessibility checks remain pending.

For an empty route result (F23), **Show all matching buses** removes only the optional Accessible tab filter when other matching journeys exist. Its disclosure explains that unknown access/barrier options may appear. Explicit trip wheelchair/step-free needs still constrain All/Fastest and route selection. **Review trip requirements** opens a separate review with the currently effective needs. Turning them off requires **Apply to this search**, may expose unknown/barrier services, and never changes the account's saved preferences. **Keep requirements** cancels without changing results. This recovery does not establish accessibility or repair missing evidence; riders still need to inspect route evidence before choosing a journey.
